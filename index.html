What's this?

CUDHacks is a student hackathon about using AI on problems the UAE actually has: government services, cities and sustainability, and the people who live here. We care less about how many ideas get pitched and more about whether a real organisation would want to test what you built. Teams mix CS and Cybersecurity students.

Computer Science

You lead the build

Data pipelines, models, back ends, front ends. You turn a challenge statement into a prototype that actually runs when the judge tries it.

Cybersecurity

You make it trustworthy

Threat models, privacy, safe data handling, abuse cases. A tool for residents or government only gets piloted if people can trust it, and that part is yours.

01

Digital UAE

Government services · Accessibility · Digital trust

Make UAE government services simpler, cut down on admin work, and protect residents, businesses and public services from digital threats.

Challenge statements
  • Government Service NavigatorAn assistant that helps residents find the right service, check whether they're eligible, and see which documents they need.
  • Document Pre-CheckSpots missing attachments in a permit or certificate application before it's submitted.
  • Government Website AuditorFinds broken links, accessibility problems and outdated information on public-service sites.
  • UAE Scam DetectorFlags suspicious job offers, phishing links and impersonation attempts.
  • SME Cybersecurity AssistantHelps small UAE businesses catch invoice fraud and basic security risks.
  • Secure AI Agent MonitorEnforces the actions an AI agent is allowed to take and flags risky behaviour in a simulated government workflow.
Or propose your ownAny government-service, accessibility or cybersecurity problem in the UAE.
CSAssistants, crawlers, document parsing, front end
CyberFraud and phishing signals, agent permissions, privacy

NoteThese are suggested concepts. We're not saying the UAE has no existing solutions to these problems. Show what your approach does better. You don't need paid AI credits either: free and open-source tools are enough for full marks.

10

FAQ

Q.01Who can join?
CS and Cybersecurity students. Every team of four has people from both. CS leads the build and Cyber makes sure it's safe and trustworthy enough to pilot.
Q.02I don't have a full team. Can I still register?
Yes. Sign up on your own or as a pair and we'll match you with people from the other discipline before the event.
Q.03Do we pick our challenge?
Yes. Choose any challenge statement from the three tracks, or propose your own problem as long as it's relevant to the UAE. You'll confirm your choice at the online challenge briefing on Friday morning.
Q.04Do we need to be on campus both days?
No. Day 1 (Fri 19 March) is online, so you can build from anywhere. Day 2 (Sat 20 March) is in person at CUD, City Walk, for the final build, live judging, the finals showcase and awards, so plan to be there.
Q.05Do we have to use real government data?
No, but it helps your impact score. The open data portals above are a good place to start. Some datasets need an access request, so if you can't get the real thing, simulated or sample data is fine as long as you say so in your demo.
Q.06Do I need paid AI tools or API keys?
No. Free and open-source models and tools are enough to get full marks.
Q.07Can we start coding early?
The challenge statements are public now, so research the problem and explore the data as much as you like. Project code has to be written during the event.
Q.08How are winners picked?
Five criteria, each scored 0–3 live: UAE impact (30%), working prototype (25%), tech & security (20%), feasibility (15%) and pitch (10%). The full rubric is above.
Q.09What happens after the hackathon?
Top teams present at the finals showcase. About 30 days later, the strongest 2–3 teams get a follow-up demo with interested organisations, and where a partner sees a real use case we'll try to arrange a small, supervised pilot.
Q.10What should I bring?
For Saturday on campus: laptop, charger, student ID. We'll handle food and Wi-Fi.
